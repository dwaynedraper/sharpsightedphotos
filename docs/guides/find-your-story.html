Hi, I'm Dean.

Almost everyone tells me the same thing the first time we talk. "I hate every photo of myself."

I get it. Most portraits ask you to be someone else for an hour. Stand here. Chin down. Now smile. You walk away with a picture of a stranger who happens to have your face.

I do it differently. You tell me your story. I build a scene around it. Then you live it while I photograph you being you.

Two kinds of story

Every session lands in one of two places.

Neither one is better. Most people don't know which one they are until we talk, and that's fine.

Direction one

Story to tell

You have something to say. Maybe you just finished something hard. Maybe you're starting something new. Maybe you want your kids to know who you were at 40.

These sessions are built around that one message and the scenes that carry it.

Direction two

Seen as you are

You don't need a big message. You just want photos that finally look like you.

These sessions are built around your everyday world, and the moments you forget I'm there.

Not sure which one you are? Good. That's exactly what the Discovery Hour is for.

This week

Notice. Don't rehearse.

Over the next week, pay attention to a few things. You don't have to write anything down. Just notice.

  • 1Where you exhale. The places where your shoulders drop without you trying.
  • 2What you reach for. The mug, the guitar, the wrench, the jacket you refuse to throw away.
  • 3What you wear when nobody's grading you. Not your best outfit. Your favorite one.
  • 4Who gets your real laugh. And what your face does when they get it.
  • 5When you feel most awake. Sunrise people and midnight people both have great light.
  • 6The thing you'd talk about for an hour. If someone finally let you.

That's it. Bring whatever stuck with you. We'll take it from there.

Before we meet

Bring a few phone photos.

Snap a few pictures before the Discovery Hour. They don't need to be good. They're for me, not for Instagram.

  • A place that feels like yours
  • An object that's been with you for years
  • An outfit you feel great in
  • Anyone or anything you might want in the frame. A pet, a car, your tools, your people.

If you have something to say

Some people show up with a message already in their chest. If that's you, bring it rough. Don't polish it, and don't try to make it sound good. We'll find the words together.

If nothing comes to mind, that's an answer too. Being seen is plenty.
